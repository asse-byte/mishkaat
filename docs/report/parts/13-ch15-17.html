15.2.1 Development method

Two characteristics of how the work proceeded are visible in the repository and are worth recording, because they explain the shape of the system.

The project pivoted once, early and completely. An initial mobile application built with Expo and React Native reached a working MVP — authentication, dashboards, and CRUD for students, teachers and finance — and was then abandoned in favour of a web application. The current PWA delivery (§12.5) is the resolution of that decision: installable on a phone without a store, from one code base.

Defects were fixed at their root and recorded at the site. Non-obvious decisions carry an inline record stating what the previous behaviour was and why it was wrong. This report draws on those records directly, and they are the reason it can state, for each mechanism, the specific failure it answers rather than a general rationale. The same discipline produced the rule that a leak is closed in the module that decides scope rather than in the endpoint where it was noticed — after patching endpoint by endpoint demonstrably failed.

Chapter 17Conclusion

Mishkaat set out to do two things: to give a Qur'an memorisation centre a single, auditable system for its academic, administrative and financial work, and to make the quality of memorisation — not merely its quantity — measurable, comparable and explainable. Both are delivered in the system documented here, and the second is what distinguishes it from a school-administration product with Arabic labels.

The engineering position that the system takes, and which this report has traced through every chapter, can be stated in five sentences.

  1. Authorisation is a design problem, not a set of checks. Seventeen data leaks came from one root cause — a system that knew about centres but not about circles — and patching them individually returned them with the next endpoint. Defining the four scope levels in one module, gating every student identifier through one function, and asserting the boundary with tests that look for leakage rather than for success is what actually closed them.
  2. Derived values must not be stored. Every metric, rank and forecast is a function of the session records at the moment of the query, which is what makes a student's position explainable by pointing at their recitations. A stored metric would diverge from its sources at the first correction and take the explicability of the whole motivation layer with it.
  3. Uniqueness that matters belongs in the database. A check followed by a write is not atomic, and the duplicate it fails to prevent is not a cosmetic duplicate in a list: it is a salary paid twice, a certificate issued twice, a metric inflated against the truth.
  4. Every administrative act is fallible, and money is voided rather than erased. A system with no correction path forces its users to work around it, and the records then stop describing reality — which is the failure the software was adopted to fix.
  5. Claims must be proportionate to evidence. The forecast returns its method, its training size and its fit, and no confidence figure, because none has been measured. A coefficient that contradicts the domain is zeroed rather than inverted. A metric with no data returns “no data”, not zero. A system that tells a parent something it cannot support loses the standing it needs to say anything at all.

What remains is honest and specific. The forecasting model is implemented and quality-sensitive but its accuracy is unvalidated; the analytical constants are provisional by declaration and await calibration against real memorisation data; there is no automated front-end testing and no measured performance envelope; and notifications do not yet reach a guardian who is not looking at the application. These are stated in Chapter 16 with the work each would require, because a report that presents a system as complete is less useful than one that says precisely where the edges are.

The system is, on the evidence assembled in this report, ready for supervised production use in a centre whose operator completes the onboarding checklist of §14.5 — and it is ready to be measured, which is the precondition for the calibration work that comes next.
