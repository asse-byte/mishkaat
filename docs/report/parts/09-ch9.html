Chapter 9The Analytical Engine

This chapter specifies the part of Mishkaat that distinguishes it from a records system: the layer that converts recitation and attendance rows into measures of academic quality, a completion forecast, and a motivation structure. Every formula, constant and design constraint is stated, together with the reason it takes the form it does.

9.1 Provenance and scientific basis

The analytical layer derives from the requirements specification of an academic project, Halaqtna (CPIT-498, King Abdulaziz University), and was carried into Mishkaat with its requirement identifiers preserved so that the mapping can be audited rather than reconstructed. The error taxonomy rests on the observation — recorded in the literature on Qur'an memorisation assessment, including the classification of Hassan and Zailaini — that an error recorded as free text cannot be compared either between students or across time.

A statement about the status of these numbers The source classifies every constant in this chapter as provisional: the ordering of severity comes from teacher interviews and from the published classification, but the numeric values are a design choice to be calibrated against real data. They are therefore held in configuration and are environment-tunable (§9.8), not buried inside a function — which is where an earlier generation of error weights (4.0 / 1.5 / 2.0) in fact lived, inside a single prediction endpoint. No accuracy claim is made anywhere in this system, and §9.4 explains what is returned instead of one.

9.2 The weighted error taxonomy

Four error classes, ordered from most to least severe. The recitation stores only the class code and a count; the weight lives in one module and is never written to a record.

Table 9.2 — Error classes
CodeClassWeightDefinition

انقطاع الحفظ		0.85The student stopped and could not continue without prompting.
LNK_ERRLinking error
خطأ في الربط		0.50The student moved to the wrong verse or the wrong position.

خطأ تجويد		0.25A fault in articulation point, elongation or stopping.

تصحيح ذاتي		0.05The student erred and corrected themselves without prompting.

Why exactly four. The teacher tags errors on a phone during the circle. A long list is not used; four classes group the linguistic distinctions into practical severity bands.

Why the weight is not stored. Storing the weight with the error would make later recalibration incapable of repairing historical data, and the same error would carry different weights in different rows depending on when it was recorded. This is the removal of a transitive dependency, and it is the reason the taxonomy is served to the client by an endpoint (GET /api/performance/error-types) rather than duplicated in the front end.

9.2.1 Reading historical records

Sessions recorded before the taxonomy carry three free counters. Ignoring them would reset every metric to zero and discard the centre's whole history — worse than a declared approximation. They are therefore mapped:

Table 9.3 — Legacy counter mapping
Legacy counterMapped classReasoning
mistakes_countMEM_GAPAn error that required the teacher to intervene.
tajweed_errors_countTAJ_ERRDirect correspondence.
hesitations_countSLF_CRTHesitation without a settled error.

The ratio among the old weights was 4.0 : 2.0 : 1.5 and among the new is 0.85 : 0.25 : 0.05 — the ordering is preserved and the separation widened. No claim is made that the two scales are equivalent. Where a session carries error_tags, the taxonomy takes precedence absolutely and the legacy counters are ignored, so a session written by both paths is never counted twice.

One distinction carries real weight: an empty tag map means “the teacher considered all four classes and found none” — perfect precision — while an absent field means the record predates tagging and yields no precision value at all. Conflating the two would place every student with a pre-taxonomy history at the bottom of the precision board on a judgement that was never made.

9.3 The five performance metrics

All metrics are per page. Mishkaat records an āyah range; where a teacher enters an explicit page count it is used, otherwise pages are derived as 6,236 āyāt ÷ 604 pages ≈ 10.32 āyāt per page. The derivation is a declared approximation, not a measurement.

9.3.1 Mastery

Mastery = 100 × ( 1 − min( 1, D / dmax ) ),   D = Σsessions Σclasses (count × weight) ⁄ Σsessions pages dmax = 3.0 weighted errors per page — the density at which mastery reaches zero. D is computed over the totals, not as a mean of per-session means: a half-page session with one error must not weigh as much as a ten-page session with one error.

9.3.2 Momentum

Mw = α · Pw + (1 − α) · Mw+1,   α = 0.4 Pw is the number of new-memorisation pages in the week w weeks ago; the recursion runs from the oldest week forward to the present, so the most recent week carries the greatest weight. Review sessions are excluded. Empty weeks enter the computation as genuine zeros, not as gaps to be skipped — skipping them would reward interruption.

Smoothing is deliberate: a raw thirty-day total — which is what the system previously computed — jumps after a holiday week and then collapses, and any forecast built on it oscillates with it.

9.3.3 Precision

Precision = 100 × ( 1 − severe / total ) computed over tagged sessions only. severe counts errors whose weight ≥ 0.5 (that is, MEM_GAP and LNK_ERR); total counts all tagged errors. Returns 100 when there are tagged sessions with no errors, and None when nothing has been tagged.

Legacy counters are excluded here on principle: their mapping assumes every generic “mistake” is a memorisation gap. That assumption is acceptable for density, where magnitude matters and ordering is preserved, and unacceptable here, where the ratio between severity bands is the measurement.

9.3.4 Consistency

Consistency = 100 × attended ⁄ min(8, sessions recorded) over the most recent eight scheduled sessions. Present, late and excused all count as attendance; only an unexcused absence does not.

9.3.5 Review depth

Review depth = min( 200, 100 × review pages ⁄ new pages )  over the last 28 days Capped at 200%: a student who reviews twice what they memorise has reached the maximum this indicator measures. Without the cap, a student who stopped memorising entirely and kept reviewing would top the board on an unbounded ratio.

9.3.6 The legacy composite score

An older composite score survives, used by the honour roll and the rankings screen:

Score = clamp[0,100]( 0.4·H + 0.3·R + 0.2·A − 0.1·E ) H and R are the mean qualitative evaluation of new and review sessions on the scale excellent = 100, good = 80, acceptable = 60, needs improvement = 40 (defaulting to 80 where absent); A is mean attendance on present = 100, late = 80, excused = 60, absent = 0 (defaulting to 100); E is the count of recorded mistakes.

It is documented here because it is live, and because its history is instructive: the same formula was written out twice, in the honour roll and in the ranking, and the two copies had already drifted — one used a thirty-day window while the other used ninety under a variable named thirty_days_ago. It is now one function, so any future change applies to both screens. Its performance history is equally instructive: it previously issued two queries per student — two thousand round trips on every home-page load in a centre of a thousand students — and now issues two queries in total.

9.4 Completion forecasting

9.4.1 What is different

The previous forecast regressed accumulated āyāt against elapsed days: quantity alone. Two students with identical counts received identical dates even when one missed half the sessions and erred on every page. The contribution of the present model is that recitation quality, not only quantity, affects the projected date.

9.4.2 Features and model

Table 9.5 — Model features
FeatureUnitRequired signRationale
Momentumpages/week+1Memorising more does not slow completion.
Weighted error densityerrors/page−1Erring more does not accelerate completion.
Attendance rate0–1+1Attending more does not slow completion.

The target is the number of new-memorisation pages produced in a week. The model is ordinary least squares with an intercept, solved from the normal equations (XᵀX)β = Xᵀy by Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting — implemented in plain Python, because the server is already Python and no numerical dependency is warranted for a 4×4 system.

9.4.3 Training-row construction

The model is trained on the whole centre's history, not one student's: three features cannot be fitted from one student's record without overfitting. Each (student, week) pair is one row. Crucially:

for each student with ≥ 3 sessions:
    for each week w after the student's first session:
        features ← computed from sessions STRICTLY BEFORE the start of week w
                   (momentum, error density, attendance rate)
        target   ← new-memorisation pages recorded DURING week w
        emit row only if ≥ 2 prior sessions exist

Computing the features over the whole period instead would leak the target into its own inputs and make R² an illusion. A minimum of 12 training rows is required before a model is fitted at all.

9.4.4 Sign constraint

A regression on a small centre's data can return a positive coefficient for error density: students who read more pages accumulate more errors, so the model captures correlation rather than causation. Left alone, the system would tell a guardian that their child's errors are bringing their completion date forward — which destroys the credibility of the prediction however good R² is. Any coefficient contradicting its known direction is therefore set to zero, not inverted: the system refuses a claim that contradicts the domain, and it does not assert the opposite without evidence.

9.4.5 Prediction, guards and honesty

weekly pages = β₀ + β₁·momentum + β₂·error density + β₃·attendance rate accepted only if 0.05 ≤ prediction ≤ max(4 × max(momentum, 1), 20). Outside that band — or when no model could be fitted — the estimate falls back to the student's own momentum, and the response says so.

Every forecast returns the method used (trained_model or momentum_fallback), the number of training rows, the coefficient of determination and the model version (mishkaat-lr-3feat-1.0). No confidence figure is returned, because none has been measured. The source specification classifies its “80% of forecasts within ±2 weeks” target as a design goal to be evaluated later, and this system does not present a design goal as a result.

Projection to dates is then arithmetic: with 604 pages over 30 parts (≈ 20.13 pages per Juz'), the remaining pages of the current Juz' and of the whole Qur'an are divided by the daily rate. A projection further away than sixty years returns no date at all rather than a figure that would merely mislead. The model is cached per centre for one hour and invalidated when a new recitation is recorded; the cache is per worker process and explicitly advisory.

9.5 The motivation layer

9.5.1 Four leaderboards, not one

The design premise, which came from the teachers themselves, is that a single ranking demotivates precisely those who most need encouragement: a student who appears at the bottom every week learns to ignore it. Mishkaat therefore publishes four independent boards:

Table 9.6 — The four leaderboards
BoardUnitWho leads it
Momentum الزخمpages/weekWhoever memorises most.
Precision الدقّة%Whoever makes the lightest errors.
Consistency الانتظام%Whoever never misses a session.
Review depth عمق المراجعة%Whoever consolidates what they have memorised.

The slow student who never misses leads consistency; the student who memorises little and reviews much leads review depth. All four derive from the same session records as the metrics — not from a parallel points system — so a student's position on any board can always be explained by pointing at their recitations and attendance. For a student or guardian the board is scoped to their own circle (§3.6, E1), which supplies peers to compare against without opening anyone's file.

9.5.4 The experience-point ledger

Points accrue at 10 per page recited and 5 per attended session, plus badge awards. Every award is a row in xp_ledger; rows are never updated or deleted. The unique index on (student_id, reason, source_id) is what makes awarding idempotent: re-submitting an attendance roll or reprocessing a session grants nothing twice, and the resulting duplicate-key error is success, not failure. Attendance uses student:date as its source key precisely so that correcting the roll does not pay twice. XP rates were chosen so that attendance alone cannot top a ranking.

9.6 Derived instruments

9.6.1 The journey map

For a student, “you have memorised 143 pages” does not say where they stand in the mushaf. The journey endpoint returns all thirty parts, each with a completion percentage and a state — done, active or todo — together with pages reviewed in the last thirty days, so that settled memorisation is visibly distinguished from memorisation being actively maintained.

9.6.2 The daily review portion

Each student's review portion is derived from their own memorised volume:

cycle days = ⌈ memorised parts ⁄ rate ⌉,   day index = (today − epoch) mod cycle days,   portion = parts [ day index · rate + 1 … ] rate defaults to one part per day — the customary rule that a ḥāfiẓ completes a full revision cycle within a month — and is adjustable per student by the teacher. A student with fewer than half a part memorised receives no portion: none is invented.

Two properties follow from deriving the portion from the date rather than from stored state: nothing can be “forgotten to update”, and every person who opens the screen on the same day sees the same portion. The fixed epoch (Saturday 3 January 2026, the start of a study week) keeps the cycle stable across reloads. Paired with it is the question the teacher actually asks — who has reviewed today and who is behind.

9.6.3 The centre activity index

Supervisory ranking by head-count rewards a centre that enrolled a hundred students and then abandoned the system over a centre where twenty students work daily. Count measures size; movement measures use. The index therefore measures transactions in a window, normalised by centre size:

score = Σk wk · min( 1, nk ⁄ ( ek · students · days ) ) · 100 with weights w = { recitations 0.45, attendance 0.30, finance 0.15, enrolment 0.10 } and per-student-per-day expectations e = { 0.7, 0.9, 0.05, 0.02 }. Recitation is weighted highest because it is the centre's actual work; finance signals a live administration rather than teaching.

Scores map to four bands — intensive (≥ 75), regular (≥ 45), intermittent (≥ 15), weak (> 0) — plus “no activity”. The response carries counts, the per-signal breakdown, the last activity timestamp and days since; it carries no student names and no student data. Supervision learns that a centre is working without reading anyone's file.

9.7 Invariant design decisions

Table 9.9 — Decisions not to be reversed without a stated reason
#DecisionWhy
1No derived value is stored.A stored metric diverges from its source rows at the first correction or void, and the leaderboard then ceases to be explainable by reference to sessions — the property the entire motivation layer rests on.
2Weights live in one place and are never stored with an error.The weights are provisional and will be recalibrated; stored weights would make recalibration unable to repair history.
3None means “no data”, not zero.A new student's mastery is not zero, and listing them at zero both wrongs them and distorts the board.
4Precision is never computed from legacy counters.They carry no class, and their mapping assumes every error is a memorisation gap — sound for density, unsound for a severity ratio. The empty map is tagged (perfect); an absent field is not.
5Sign constraints on model coefficients.A system that tells a guardian that their child's errors accelerate completion loses all credibility, whatever its R².
6A badge is granted once and never withdrawn.It records an event, not a state.
7The unique key on the XP ledger is load-bearing.Without it any repeat inflates a balance without limit.
8Printed output uses fixed colour values, never design-system tokens.Tokens invert in dark mode, so printing at night would produce pale text on white paper.
